Final Research Summary — GNN-DRL-PSO for QoS-Aware Single-Cloud Service Composition
==================================================================================
Generated: 2026-02-XX (rerun with `python main.py --stage report` to refresh)

Research question
-----------------
"Does incorporating GNN-based service-dependency representations into a
DRL-PSO pipeline improve QoS-aware service composition in a
single-cloud environment?"

Experimental setup
------------------
* Dataset      : QWS Dataset v2.0 — 2,505 real web services × 9 QoS
                 attributes (2 rows dropped because their WSDL URL
                 contained an embedded comma).
* Split        : deterministic 80/20 by service (seed=42).
                   Train: 2,006 services      Test: 501 services.
* QoS fitness  : normalized weighted aggregation (weights sum to 1.0);
                 per-attribute aggregation rules for a sequential
                 workflow — SUM for latency/response_time, PRODUCT for
                 reliability/availability/successability, MIN for
                 throughput, MEAN for compliance/best_practices/
                 documentation.
* Workflows    : length 5 abstract tasks; task tag = derived Service
                 Classification (Platinum/Gold/Silver/Bronze), obtained
                 from the published WsRF quartile rule fitted on the
                 training partition only.
* GNN          : 2-layer GraphSAGE, hidden=64, embed=32; pretrained
                 with a graph-autoencoder objective (30 epochs) on the
                 training graph.
* PPO          : lr=3e-4, γ=0.99, GAE(λ)=0.95, clip=0.2, entropy=0.01,
                 hidden=128, rollout_size=4, 4 update epochs, batch=64.
* PSO          : 20 particles × 15 iterations; w=0.7, c1=c2=1.5;
                 discrete swap-probability velocity; every particle is
                 constrained to a syntactically valid composition.
* Budget       : 4 configurations × 5 seeds × 50 training episodes.

Baseline vs Proposed
--------------------
Baseline    : DRL_PSO      — no graph structure; service embeddings
                             are a fixed random-orthogonal projection
                             of raw features into R^32.
Proposed    : GNN_DRL_PSO  — service embeddings come from a
                             pretrained GraphSAGE encoder over the
                             service dependency graph (intra-class kNN
                             + inter-class kNN on QoS similarity).

Fairness controls
-----------------
Both configurations share identical seeds, dataset split, normalizer,
QoS weights, workflow generator, PPO/PSO hyperparameters, state MLP
architecture, and evaluation protocol. The only differences are
(a) presence of graph-derived service embeddings, and
(b) presence of PSO refinement in the specific ablation cell.

    GNN_DRL_PSO      : 0.5927 ± 0.0090      (proposed)

Computational overhead
----------------------
DRL inference / episode        DRL_PSO : 0.0169 s   GNN_DRL_PSO : 0.0145 s
PSO / episode                  DRL_PSO : 0.0372 s   GNN_DRL_PSO : 0.0368 s
Mean total per seed            DRL_PSO : 12.6 s     GNN_DRL_PSO : 19.0 s
(Additional 19-vs-13 s per seed comes from 30 GNN-autoencoder pretrain
epochs; runtime PPO inference is essentially unchanged.)

Ablation findings
-----------------
* PSO is the largest single contributor: adding PSO to DRL improves
  mean QoS by +9.2 percentage-points (0.541 -> 0.591).
* GNN alone (GNN_DRL) actually underperformed DRL alone (0.499 vs
  0.541) on this dataset in this training budget. One plausible
  explanation: the graph autoencoder objective does not directly
  optimise for composition quality, so its embeddings are informative
  about neighbourhood structure but not necessarily about the
  composition reward.  PSO can absorb this because it re-optimises
  the composition regardless of initial choices.
* When PSO is present the GNN change becomes marginal (+0.0015).

Research interpretation (auto-generated)
----------------------------------------
There is insufficient statistical evidence that GNN-based service-
dependency representations change QoS relative to the DRL-PSO
baseline in the tested regime.  This matches outcome (B) explicitly
enumerated in the research plan: "GNN provides a small but
statistically insignificant improvement." Note that p > 0.05 does NOT
prove the methods are equivalent — the current experimental budget
(5 seeds × 50 episodes) simply does not provide enough evidence
either way. A larger budget and/or a joint GNN-DRL training regime
(configurable via `gnn.training_mode: joint`) would be needed to
tighten the confidence interval.

Limitations
-----------
1. Service split, not workflow split.  Workflows are synthesized from
   Service Classification tags.  Different workflow-generation seeds
   separate train and test, so no test workflow was ever seen during
   training, but the two partitions have no natural
   task/query separation.
2. GNN pretraining uses a graph autoencoder objective that is not
   directly aligned with the QoS composition reward.  The joint
   training mode (`gnn.training_mode: joint`) is implemented but not
   used in the default report.
3. Non-GNN configurations use a fixed random-orthogonal projection as
   their service encoder.  A learned linear encoder might close some
   of the gap; this is deliberately kept as a baseline extension.
4. 50 episodes × 5 seeds is intentionally modest for a first
   experiment.  Section "Reproducibility" in README.md documents how
   to scale the budget without touching any code.
